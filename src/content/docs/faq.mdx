title: FAQ
description: Common questions about Bancuh DNS.
---

## How much does it cost?

Nothing. There's no sign-up and no paid tier. The servers are run out of pocket
as a personal project.

## How does DNS ad blocking work?

Before your device can load anything from the internet, it looks up the
server's address with DNS, like looking up a number in a phone book. Bancuh DNS
answers those lookups normally, except for names on its
[blocklist](/filtering/). For those, it answers with an address that goes
nowhere (`0.0.0.0`), so the ad, tracker or site never loads.

Because it works at the DNS level, it covers every app on a device, and every
device on a network if you set it on your router, including ones where you
can't install an ad blocker such as smart TVs and consoles.

## Who is it for?

Anyone who wants fewer ads and a safer internet, without installing anything.
It's especially suited to families: set it on your home router and every
device, including children's, gets ad blocking, malware protection, an adult
content filter and enforced SafeSearch.

## Can I turn off some of the blocking?

No. Everyone gets the same filter. If you need different rules, for example to
allow VPNs or crypto sites, you can [run your own copy](/self-hosting/) with
your own blocklist.

## Could you offer a lighter, ads-only server?

Not for now. Every server runs the same configuration, because running a
second filter in each location would double the cost. Protection for the
whole family, with nothing to configure, is also what sets Bancuh DNS apart;
there are plenty of good ads-only services if that's what you need.

## Why are Google Ads and Google Analytics blocked?

Because people use Bancuh DNS expecting ads and analytics to be blocked. A few
sites and apps misbehave without them. For those, use the
[per-device workaround](/troubleshooting/#using-a-site-thats-blocked).

## Why is there no "blocked" page?

A blocked domain simply doesn't resolve, so there's no server to show a page.
Showing one would mean answering with the address of a Bancuh server, which
would then have to present a certificate for sites it doesn't own. Browsers
would show a security warning instead. Use your
[query logs](/logs/) to see what was blocked.

## Do you log my browsing?

Query logs are kept in memory for 10 minutes so you can see your own lookups,
and are then deleted. Nobody else can see yours. See
[Query logs & privacy](/logs/) for the details.

Ideally there would be no logs at all, but without them there's no way to
work out why a site or app broke, especially on phones where there are no
developer tools. Ten minutes, visible only to you, is the compromise.

## Are my lookups passed on to Google or Cloudflare?

No. Early on, the servers forwarded lookups to public DNS services. Since
2024, each server resolves names itself, asking each website's own DNS
servers directly.

## Should I use a browser ad blocker instead?

Use both if you can. Browser extensions such as uBlock Origin can remove ads
that DNS can't, like YouTube's, and tidy up the empty spaces left behind. But
they only work in that one browser. Bancuh DNS covers other browsers, apps and
devices that have no extensions, and a whole home network at once.

## Why not use a hosts file?

A hosts file blocks domains on one device without any DNS server, and works
offline. But it has to be kept up to date by hand, needs admin or root access
(which rules out most phones), has to be set up on every device, and is hard to
undo if something breaks. Bancuh DNS gives you the same effect with one
setting, and updates itself every day.

## How is it different from AdGuard DNS or NextDNS?

Those are excellent services with dashboards, per-user settings and paid
tiers. Bancuh DNS is simpler: free, no account, one strict family filter for
everyone, and completely open source, so you can
[run the same thing yourself](/self-hosting/).

## Will it slow down my internet?

Usually it does the opposite, because pages don't wait for ads and trackers to
load. Choose the [closest server](/servers/) to keep lookups fast.

## Can I use it at a school or business?

Yes, but there's no uptime guarantee, and very large networks behind a single
IP address may hit the per-IP limits. See
[large networks](/troubleshooting/#large-networks). For anything important,
[host your own](/self-hosting/).

## Can you add a server in my country?

Maybe. New locations are added where there are enough users to justify the
cost. Open an [issue on GitHub](https://github.com/ragibkl/adblock-dns-server/issues)
to ask, and in the meantime use the closest [existing server](/servers/).

## Can I suggest a blocklist?

Yes. Open an [issue on GitHub](https://github.com/ragibkl/adblock-dns-server/issues)
with a link to the list. Much of the current list came from user suggestions.

## What does "Bancuh" mean?

It's Malay for *to mix*, as in stirring up a drink. It started as a domain
name that happened to be available, and users began calling the service
"Bancuh DNS". It turned out to fit: the blocklist is a mix of dozens of
others. More in [About Bancuh DNS](/about/).

## Something doesn't work. How do I get help?

Start with [troubleshooting](/troubleshooting/). If that doesn't help,
[open an issue on GitHub](https://github.com/ragibkl/adblock-dns-server/issues).
